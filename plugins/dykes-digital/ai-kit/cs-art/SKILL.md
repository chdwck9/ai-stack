# CS Art

Generate consistent accompanying cover art for Career Project Evidence pages. The image should communicate the project's distinctive system, problem, mechanism or outcome through a strong central illustration. It is an accompanying illustration, not a complete architecture diagram or presentation slide.

## Steps

Copy these steps verbatim into the task list before starting. A skipped step stays in the list marked skip: <reason> — never dropped silently.

1. **Understand.** Identify the project's core subject, mechanism and distinctive visual metaphor. Prefer concrete product, technical, data, commercial or workflow imagery over generic business imagery.

1. **Reference.** Inspect existing Career Project Evidence cover art when available and preserve the established visual language. Do not reinvent the visual system for each project.

1. **Compose.** Use a 1:1 square canvas with the primary visual focal point positioned on the centre horizontal row. The composition must survive both Notion gallery cropping and full-page viewing.

1. **Style.** Use a #1E1E1E background and white hand-drawn/sketch/chalk illustration only. No accent colours, coloured fills, logos in brand colours, or other coloured elements.

1. **Illustrate.** Put the project's central object/system in the centre. Use a small number of supporting visual elements to suggest the problem, mechanism, relationships or outcome. Simplify aggressively: communicate the idea, do not document every component, metric or process step.

1. **Text.** Include some short handwritten labels or annotations where they improve comprehension. Text should support the imagery, not compete with it. Avoid titles, paragraphs, exhaustive labels, metric dumps and diagram-like documentation.

1. **Generate.** Use the image-generation tool and iterate against composition, simplicity and subject requirements rather than accepting a generic first pass.

1. **Unslop.** Apply $unslop as a final visual cleanup pass. Remove generic, decorative, repetitive or over-explained visual elements and unnecessary text without changing the project's meaning or visual system. If the result fails the visual quality gate, regenerate rather than decorating around the problem.

1. **Evaluate.** Check: square aspect ratio; centre-row focal point; central subject; #1E1E1E background; white-only sketch language; strong subject-specific imagery; a few useful words; restrained information density; usable in both gallery and page view.

## Examples

- **Data platform:** central data/lakehouse/pipeline object with a few ingestion sources and analytics/AI outputs. Avoid a full architecture map.

- **Migration:** central target infrastructure with a simple transition from source infrastructure, plus a few cues for scale, global access or outcomes. Avoid enumerating every service and migration stage.

- **Pricing & packaging:** central pricing/package transformation with a few cues for customer fit, usage/economics and differentiated tiers. Avoid turning the cover into a pricing model or feature matrix.

## Boundaries

- Never default to a title-led cover.

- Never make the composition primarily horizontal if this pushes the focal point outside the centre row.

- Never place the main subject only at the top or bottom.

- Never use generic corporate stock-art aesthetics.

- Never make text the primary visual element.

- Never produce a full technical architecture diagram when an accompanying illustration will communicate the case study.

- Never overload the image with components, labels, metrics or process steps.

- Never use colour. The illustration is white sketch/chalk linework on #1E1E1E.

- Never invent technical components that materially change the project's story.

- Preserve the established visual system across covers.

- If the project has a distinctive architecture, workflow, product or mechanism, represent that rather than abstracting it into generic technology imagery.

## Output

- Format: square 1:1 image.

- Background: #1E1E1E.

- Illustration: white sketch/chalk linework only.

- Composition: central focal point on the centre horizontal row.

- Information density: simple accompanying illustration, not exhaustive diagram.

- Text: a few short labels/annotations; no title by default.

- Subject: project-specific central visual metaphor/system.

- Quality gate: all composition, style, simplicity, colour and subject requirements must pass before delivery.

## Next steps

Generate the cover, apply $unslop, then revise or regenerate if the central composition, simplicity, subject specificity, visual hierarchy or white-only treatment is wrong.
